# WGS-Urban_Rural_UR-BCM

Geography and intestinal parasites may jointly shape the gut microbiome, yet their relative 
contributions remain unclear, especially in underrepresented areas, such as Latin America.
Using molecular detection for nine intestinal parasites, we sampled Colombian children from
26 urban Medellín (n=48) and rural Unguía (n=32), who were classified as parasite-negative,
27 monoparasitized, or polyparasitized. MetaPhlAn 4 and HUMAnN were used for species-level
28 taxonomic and functional profiling. Medellín participants had higher Shannon diversity than
29 Unguía participants (P=0.0046). Geography was the strongest determinant of community
30 structure (PERMANOVA pseudo-F=4.998, P=0.001), although regional dispersion also
31 differed (PERMDISP P=0.0002). Ascaris lumbricoides and Trichuris trichiura detection were
32 associated with community composition (P=0.010 and P=0.013, respectively). Whereas
33 Giardia duodenalis, Blastocystis species, and polyparasitism were not. Dispersion differed by
34 Trichuris status (P=0.030). Neither individual parasite presence nor polyparasitism
35 significantly altered alpha diversity, and parasite loads were not correlated with diversity
36 indices. Differential taxonomic signatures included both characterized and unclassified
37 species-level genome bins. Functional profiling identified Ascaris as the only parasite
